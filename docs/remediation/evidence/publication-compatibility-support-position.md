## Evidence required before a bounded future claim

A separately governed future phase would need to identify, at minimum:

1. one exact immutable release subject and complete inclusion/exclusion set;
2. an Accepted release identity/version policy and separately selected version;
3. exact source, dependency, validation, evidence, security/privacy/legal, and
   limitation closure for that release subject;
4. one exact release record and attributable final-human approval;
5. publication material, channel, audience, terms, and provenance;
6. a narrowly worded compatibility scope with exact versions, targets,
   conditions, exceptions, and evidence;
7. a support commitment with scope, duration, correction, vulnerability,
   supersession, and end-of-support boundaries; and
8. channel-specific correction, withdrawal, cache/mirror, and historical-
   traceability handling.

This list is evidence orientation, not a roadmap authorization. Each item may
require a separate architecture, decision, assessment, or consequential-action
contract. None is created by REMEDIATE-001.

## Claim discipline

The following must not be inferred from Accepted documents, schema validity,
the two-evaluator reproduction record, repository visibility, issue closure,
mergeability, or a future integration of this dossier:

- `ready`, stable, production-ready, secure, compliant, certified, compatible,
  supported, published, released, maintained, or deployable;
- universal validator, implementation, Artifact Instance, or interoperability
  conformance;
- a release version, support line, maintenance duration, compatibility window,
  audience warranty, or fitness statement; or
- permission to tag, package, publish, distribute, support, withdraw, or
  deploy.

Claims remain exactly scoped, attributable, evidence-backed, version-pinned,
time-bounded, and separately authorized. Missing, stale, restricted,
conflicting, or unverifiable material must fail closed rather than be replaced
by optimism, majority, consensus, ranking, or latest-wins.

## Correction and withdrawal boundary

Because no publication channel or release set is selected, no concrete
correction or withdrawal plan can yet exist. A future plan would have to
preserve the original historical record, identify the corrected or withdrawn
subject and scope, communicate channel-specific effects, address mirrors and
caches without claiming control over them, preserve security and privacy, and
avoid silently rewriting prior claims.

This conceptual boundary performs no correction, withdrawal, deletion,
unpublishing, supersession, deprecation, or support action.
